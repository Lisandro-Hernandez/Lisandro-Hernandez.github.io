Research Overview

My research interests lie at the intersection of theoretical chemical physics, statistical mechanics, and computational engineering, with a primary focus on understanding and simulating complex molecular systems. I work on bridging microscopic quantum phenomena with macroscopic thermodynamic behavior, utilizing techniques ranging from quantum dynamics and path integral methods to semi-classical theories and open quantum systems. By applying advanced molecular dynamics, Monte Carlo methods, and enhanced sampling techniques, my research investigates the fundamental properties of matter, including the theory of phase transitions, water modeling, and hydrophobicity.

In addition to fundamental theory, my work translates molecular-level insights into practical solutions across materials modeling, process kinetics, and multi-scale transport phenomena. I am actively exploring the integration of modern computational paradigms, including physics-informed machine learning, quantum information science, and quantum computing, to advance the simulation of chemical and biological processes. This multi-disciplinary approach allows us to tackle complex challenges in multi-pathway drug transport, reaction engineering, and materials response theory.
